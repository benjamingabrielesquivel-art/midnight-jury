          The rules of the house

          How the jury sits

          1. ISealed testimony

            Each juror reads your petition through their own trade and chooses three films alone, sealed off from the others. Every case must point to a scene or a choice the filmmaker made.

          2. IICross-examination

            Each juror reads every testimony, attacks the weakest rival film, defends their own favourite, concedes one rival film and corrects any wrong facts. Full hearings only.

          3. IIIThe verdict

            The chair weighs truth, fit, evidence and range, in that order, picks five films and sets the order you watch them in.

          • Alone first. Jurors who heard each other first would agree too early. Sealed testimony keeps their appetites apart.
          • Evidence, not adjectives. "Atmospheric" proves nothing. A held shot, a sound, a cut does.
          • A chair with written rules. The loudest juror doesn't win by being loud.
          • A guard on facts. A wrong year or director gets a film thrown out, and the jurors check each other. Still check a film before you hunt it down.
